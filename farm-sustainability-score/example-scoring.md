# Example scoring (synthetic)

> Fully synthetic and illustrative. The practices, categories, weights, and points below are **made up**
> to show the *method* only. They are not the real rubric, weights or formulas.

## A miniature rubric (invented)

Say the score had just two categories, each with a weight, and a few practices worth points:

| Category (weight) | Practice | Points if followed |
|---|---|---|
| Soil health (×2) | Cover cropping | 20 |
| Soil health (×2) | Reduced tillage | 15 |
| Soil health (×2) | Organic matter testing | 10 |
| Inputs (×1) | Precision fertiliser application | 20 |
| Inputs (×1) | Integrated pest management | 15 |

## A farm's responses

The farm follows: cover cropping, organic matter testing, and precision fertiliser application.

## Working it through

1. **Raw points by category**
   - Soil health: 20 (cover cropping) + 10 (organic matter) = 30, out of a possible 45
   - Inputs: 20 (precision fertiliser) = 20, out of a possible 35

2. **Apply category weights**
   - Soil health: 30/45 × 2 = 1.33
   - Inputs: 20/35 × 1 = 0.57
   - Weighted total: 1.90 out of a possible 3.0

3. **Normalise to 0–1000**
   - 1.90 / 3.0 × 1000 = **633**

4. **Potential impact (the improvement plan)**
   - The biggest remaining gain is reduced tillage (15 pts in the ×2 category), so it tops the farm's
     prioritised action list.

5. **Verification**
   - If, say, "precision fertiliser application" is claimed but not yet verified, the score can be shown
     as verified vs claimed, so the number is honest about its evidence.

That's the shape of the method: practices → weighted points → a normalised, comparable score → a
prioritised plan, with verification. The real model is larger and the numbers are different; this just
shows how the pieces fit.
